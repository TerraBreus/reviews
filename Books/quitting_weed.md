## Quitting Weed
_By Matthew Clarke_

Though I only finished this book while already about two months clean, this book helped me not only quit in the first place, but also understand (after two months) why I shouldn't expect to go back.

The core message of this book is how addiction is a coping mechanism for emotional neglect. It tells you that there are certain feelings you are unable to live with, and that you have grown the habit
that it is better to forget than to react. Thus addiction, in this case in the form of weed, becomes a way of numbing the senses and the soul in order to "live" with yourself.

Obviously this is a fallacy. While you might think you are able to "live" in this way, really you are denying yourself to live truly. There are many forms of coping in this way and they don't have to take
the form of drugs necessarily. However, with weed there often is an aftermath and the health consequences aren't something to brush under the rug either.

This book did a very good job at showing the core "flaw" for people with a weed addiction. It also showed the many different thought patterns that can arise during abstinence. 
It also tells you the best way to change this behavior is to change your life; use your newly bought free time as an opportunity to do something new. Clarke really helps to show what not to do and
what alternatives to take.

Moreover, I felt like Clarke didn't try to preach to their audience what the right way to go about this is. Clarke wants to help, but not sell their approach as the "one and only". 
He leaves plenty of room for other approaches and emphasizes seeking help as an act of strength and not a weakness.

Highly recommend if people struggle quitting weed.

8/10
