## Adrian Mole - The Cappuccino years
_Written by Sandy Johnson_

The very first chapter of this book for some reason is extremely long making the start of this novel somewhat jerky and unpleasant. However, once I got through this "chapter", entries became a lot shorter, sometimes even less than two minutes of a read. 
Adrian Mole's diary basically became my go to read whenever I had even the littlest of free time.

As a kid I used to read Mole's diaries. Not much is left from those times except for the laughter and joy I experienced from those books. Now, more than ten years later, Adrian Mole hits in a different way.

Reading Mole's diary felt extremely personal. In no way am I what society would call a "successful person". At the age of twenty seven all I got to my name is that; 
I dropped out of college twice, moved back in with my parents, don't have a proper job, many many unfinished projects and a romantic life that leaves Stephen Hawking sound like a Casanova.

But I wouldn't call myself unhappy or "behind" on life. I get to laugh about my failures, my awkwardness, my extremely long detours and all the things that make life feel futile and strange as it is. 

And Adrian Mole just magnified it. I related to Adrian Mole's experience with the same laughter and joy as I do with my own awkwardness. Even though this book is very relevant to the time it was written in, many experiences of Mole are relatable still for today's readers.
Besides, the book is so "easy to read" that you can easily skimp over the stuff that is outdated. 

This book made me happy, it made me laugh, it made me smile. It made reading a pleasure, a small getaway of the world to live in a fictional world where one can laugh at the absurdity of life without feeling the weight of its existence.

7.5/10
