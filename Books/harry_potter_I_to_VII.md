# Harry Potter (Franchise)

Pfff... well, in six months I found myself rushing through the Harry Potter books. What started as an act of boredom turned into an obsession and joyfully reliving childhood fantasies.

I'm not too sure what to say about this franchise. I definitely enjoyed reading the three-thousand-plus pages that make up all of Rowling's work on the Boy Who Lived. 

I liked how the books start out quite thin and light. Book I and II have around two-hundred to three-hundred pages while the final book is over seven-hundred pages long.
The pacing of the entire franchise is also quite remarkable. At first the books are somewhat stand-alone but as the franchise goes on, Rowling builds upon the wizarding world
 and a more deeper storyline starts to unfold as the character dynamics develop further.

At times, the books also feel a little dragged. I got annoyed by how the first four/six books all followed somewhat of the same storyline where the climax of Voldemort 
or whatever else was happening in the book only unfolded at the end. I understand this is also how basic climax-writing works but I felt a little dissapointed knowing 
I could quite simply skip massive parts of the first half of the book and still understand the basic storyline of the book. On the other side, this also allowed for light reading.
 You don't need to know or remember every bit of detail when reading Harry Potter. 

 To give each of the books a little credit, here's a short review for every one of them.

 - **Philosophers Stone:** Cute, lighthearted and sweet. Setting of the series without diving deep into the darkness of the wizarding lore. _3/5_
 - **Chamber of Secrets:** Also quite nice. I enjoyed how to the point these books were. There was no "chit-chatting" for character development. _4/5_
 - **Prisoner of Azkaban:** Honestly don't remember this one very well. Somewhere in the back of my mind however, I feel I deeply enjoyed this one. _3/5_
 - **Goblet of Fire:** Loved this one because it combines shift from the stand-alone books towards the Harry Potter vs Voldemort Franchise while still having its own stand-alone feel. Also really liked how this tournament contributed towards the wizard lore. _4/5_
 - **Order of the Pheonix:** This one did not feel as stand-alone anymore. I can feel Rowling setting everything up for the final book. Still a good book. _3/5_
 - **Half-Blood Prince:** Still heavily setting everything up for the final book. Yet it also allowed for character-development with a little stand-alone feel due to the Book of the HBP. _3/5_
 - **The Deathly Hallows:** Awesome. I was a bit sick of the series at this point so I had to plow through it at times but was very happy I stuck with it. _5/5_

 Overall I remember the first four books the most. Partially cause those were the ones I had read as a child. The fifth and sixth book are nice but (because they were quite a lot longer) lacked their "stand-alone" feel that I liked from the other books so much.
 The final book of the series is an absolute banger and an amazing ending to a franchise. The final chapter of the book is unnecessary and can easily be skipped.

 **8/10**
